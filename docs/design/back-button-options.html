The way home, made of something

These variations on B, the coloured title-block bar, keep the bar and change its left end. The guide's desk gives the vocabulary: paper holds information, steel holds controls, a split circle is a period motif, and the Realm is a plan laid on a blotter. Each option is shown on Knowledge (teal) and on a territory (sage), at game size. Hover and press the buttons to try them.

The key plan

KnowledgeAncient era · 4 insight
Delta MarshTerritory · 2 of 3

Architects' title blocks carry a key plan: a thumbnail of the whole building with the part this sheet shows filled in. Here the button is a thumbnail of the Realm. There is one cell per territory card in tableau order, the frontier is hatched as on the board, and the territory you're in is filled. Under it is "◂ REALM" with the Esc keycap.

On hover, the plan lifts with a hard plinth shadow (§11.3's tile hover). On press, it seats.

  • + The button is a picture of where it takes you, so "click the Realm to go to the Realm" needs no explaining. On a territory it also answers "which one am I in?"
  • + It is pure drafting and paper, the guide's §11.6 site-plan world, and adds no new motif.
  • − It needs the Realm's layout: the engine already lists territories, and the UI draws the cells. With many territories, the cells must shrink or wrap.
  • − On Knowledge nothing is filled, which is honest (you're not in any territory) but tells you less.

The sheet callout

KnowledgeSheet 2 · Ancient era
Delta MarshSheet R.2 · Territory

A drawing set's reference bubble, the split circle that says "see sheet 1", with a triangle pointing back. The Realm is sheet 1. Each screen's context reads as a sheet number, so the set has an order. On hover, the disc fills like a lit lamp.

  • + It uses the split circle, a motif the guide already names (§2), and is a crisp, iconic mark that scales down well.
  • − You have to know what an architect's callout means: "R / 1" is decoration until the label explains it.
  • − Counts as the screen's one period motif, which Knowledge may want for its 12-ray burst.

The divider tab

KnowledgeAncient era · 4 insight
Delta MarshTerritory

The bar's left end is cut away to show the index tab of the Realm sheet underneath, in the board's colour, its edge slanted like a file divider. Clicking it pulls that sheet back on top. On press, the tab nudges left, the way it will slide.

  • + It shows the space: the Realm is still there underneath, and its tab is how you get it. It matches the slide-out transition.
  • + It's simple to build: a slanted panel and a label.
  • − A territory nested on Knowledge would need two tabs stepped side by side to stay honest.

The console key

KnowledgeAncient era · 4 insight
Delta MarshTerritory

A steel keycap from the desk's console, with a dual legend: "◂ REALM" as the main legend and "ESC" small in the corner, like the shifted legend on a real key. It travels and seats when pressed (§10.1), and it is a sibling of the End turn key.

  • + It's clearly a control: steel holds controls, and it reads as a button at once.
  • + Esc is part of the key's legend instead of a separate keycap.
  • − It's the least thematic of the five: a key labelled "Realm" is just a nicer button. It's also steel sitting on a paper bar, which §1 frowns on.

The drawer label

EscKnowledgeAncient era · 4 insight
EscDelta MarshTerritory

A card-catalog drawer's label holder and cup pull: a framed paper label above a half-round pull. Knowledge's techs are already index cards (§11.3), so the screen is a drawer you've pulled out, and the pull pushes it back.

  • + It's very much of 1962 and pairs with the guide's "drawer on its runners" sound.
  • − The metaphor only fits Knowledge, since a territory isn't a drawer. It edges toward the "retro" the guide rules out, and the pull's shape doesn't say "back".

Recommendation: the key plan, with the divider tab as fallback

  1. Key plan (1). It's the only option where the button itself carries information: the Realm in miniature, with your place in it marked. It belongs to the desk's drafting language without adding a motif. Label it "◂ REALM" with the Esc keycap so nobody has to decode it.
  2. When stacked (a territory opened over Knowledge), the plan still goes to the Realm, and the breadcrumb's parent ("Knowledge") stays as a text link beside the name for the one-step-back case.
  3. If the plan reads poorly at small sizes (many territories, or the Paper theme), fall back to the divider tab (3). It is just as clear about the space, cheaper to build, and has no drawing to scale.
