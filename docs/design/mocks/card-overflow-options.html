Card text overflow

spike/card-art · a hand-size card (264 × 360, with its art plate) and a tableau card (245 × 175)

With the art plate, a hand-size card has room for about six lines of rules (four with a reason strip); a tableau card about four. Most cards fit, but the long tail doesn't: Anarchy (one 380-character paragraph), techs that unlock many cards (Sailing 9 lines, Code of Laws 8), governments and civilizations that list their numbers (Theocracy, Sumer), and wonders on the tableau (Great Harbor of Tyre, Royal Road). Every option below lays out the same cards with the engine's real face text; the last card in each row is a short one for comparison. Lines are measured live, so each option fits or fails honestly. Toggle Paper / Night.

Chosen: I, with the meter cue. The engine writes tighter face text (F): one Unlocks line, figures in a ledger, and Needs, Eureka and Starts on as 14 px fine print. At rest a card keeps its art and shows whole rules, with a +N more foot when some don't fit. When the pointer rests on it for 120 ms, the text sheet slides up over the art only as far as needed; if rules are still hidden, the foot's dashed rule fills over 1.1 s and the full rules open in a printed tab beside the card, with no click. In this hand only Anarchy needs the tab. No card shrinks its type or grows, so the hand stays one even row.

The earlier proposal, G (fine print, then the art yields at rest, then OVER), is kept below for the record; I keeps the art at rest and lets it yield only while the player reads.

A · The card grows Today

What the game does now: a card is at least its size, and taller when its text needs it. Shown for reference.

For
  • Everything is readable, nothing hidden.
  • No new code.
Against
  • The hand turns into a ragged skyline; a tall card can push past the bottom of the screen.
  • The tableau row loses its grid; the Gives row in a tech's details goes uneven.

B · Smaller type

Rules step down from body (20) to body-s (17), then to 15, until they fit. Everything else stays.

For
  • Keeps the art and every word.
  • Simple: one rule, applied per card.
Against
  • Neighbouring cards print at different sizes: the hand stops reading as one series (§2, information as print).
  • 15 px rules at arm's length fail tenet 1, and Anarchy still overflows.

C · The art yields

When the rules don't fit, the art plate shrinks from 96 px to a 24 px strip through its middle (the band of the 5:2 crop), and if that isn't enough, it goes. The rules keep full size. Art is the one part of a card that is decoration, so it gives way first.

For
  • Frees 72–104 px, three or four lines, without touching the type.
  • The strip still carries the picture's colour and horizon: the card is recognisably the same.
Against
  • The cards with the most to say lose their picture, techs most of all.
  • Alone, Sailing and Anarchy still don't fit, even with no art.

D · OVER: cut at a whole rule

The library index card's convention: what doesn't fit continues "over". The card shows whole rules while they fit, then a dashed rule and a printed foot: +3 more · details I. Never a fade (§2: no gradients), never a cut through a line. The details window (click, or I) already holds the full text.

For
  • Every card keeps its size and its art.
  • Honest: says that there's more, and how much.
Against
  • What's hidden may be the line that matters (Sailing loses its eureka, Theocracy its upkeep).
  • Playing a card you can't fully read is a trap; used broadly, it's a crutch for wordy text.

E · Pull out on hover

At rest, as D. Hovered or focused, the card extends upward from its foot, like an index card drawn up out of its tray, and shows all of it; it settles back when the pointer leaves (100 ms, machined, §9). Hover the cards; the second row shows each one held out.

For
  • Full text with no click, size and art kept at rest.
  • The motion is a mechanism the desk already has (the hover lift).
Against
  • The extended card covers the board above it, and the hover lift gets a second, bigger meaning.
  • Touch and keyboard need their own trigger; dragging a card that changes height is awkward.

F · Ledger and fine print

Change the words, not the layout. The engine writes tighter face text: repeated unlocks become one Unlocks line; a government's or civilization's figures go in a ledger (label left, tabular figure right); conditions that gate a card rather than describe it (Needs, Eureka, Starts on, Built over turns) drop to a line of fine print at the foot. The details keep the long form.

For
  • Fixes the cause: Sailing goes from 8 rules to 3 plus a line of fine print, Theocracy from 6 to 2 plus a ledger.
  • Reads like a spec sheet, the period's own answer to dense information.
Against
  • Engine work (card text is generated), under TDD, and two text forms to keep in step.
  • With the art at full size, enough on its own only for Theocracy.

G · The ladder: F, then C, then D

Fine print always; the art yields only on a card whose rules still don't fit; OVER only if even that fails. In this hand Theocracy fits after F with its art; Sailing, Code of Laws and Sumer fit with their art as a strip; Anarchy loses its art and still hides three sentences. On the tableau, the tighter text without its fine print fits both wonders.

H · Hover: pull up, then pop up

At rest, as D: the art, whole rules, and a +N more foot. When the pointer rests on a card for 120 ms (a pointer passing through moves nothing), the text sheet slides up over the art, only as far as the hidden text needs, and stops (120 ms, machined). If rules are still hidden, a wait cue runs for 1.1 s and then the full rules open beside the card in a printed tab (§11.11), with no click. The pointer may move onto the tab; leaving both, Esc, or a press closes it, and a press also cancels the wait so a drag or a double-click is never blocked. Keyboard focus pulls the sheet up; I opens the tab. Cards with nothing hidden only lift, and Godot's plain tooltip goes. The text is today's engine text: Code of Laws, Theocracy and Sumer stop at the pull-up; Sailing is one rule short and opens the tab, as Anarchy does. With F's tighter text, Sailing would stop at the pull-up too. H is the hover alternative to G's second step: the art stays at rest and yields only while you look.

For
  • The hand keeps its art and its even row at rest.
  • Full text with no click, and in two steps the player can see coming.
  • Replaces the plain tooltip every card already shows, rather than adding a layer.
Against
  • The sheet moves up to 104 px on a hover, past the guide's 12 px for frequent interactions (§12 rule 4): an exception for long cards only.
  • Timers to tune (120 ms intent, 1.1 s wait), and a tab that covers part of the board.
  • No hover on touch; keyboard players rely on I.
  • One rule short (Sailing) and the tab repeats seven lines to show one; a tab of only the hidden lines ("continued") would be shorter but loses context.

I · H with F's text Chosen

H's hover (rest 120 ms, the sheet rises only as far as needed, a 1.1 s meter, then the tab) on F's tighter face text: one Unlocks line, figures in a ledger, and Needs, Eureka and Starts on as fine print under the rules. The tab still gives the full long form, as the details do. Fewer cards need the pull-up at all (Theocracy now fits at rest), the ones that do rise less (Sumer 10 px, Code of Laws 16, Sailing 40, against 39, 87 and 104 in H), and only the truly long card opens the tab.

For
  • The tab becomes rare: in this hand only Anarchy needs it.
  • The sheet rises less, so more of the art stays in view while reading.
  • The tab shows something the card can't, the long form, rather than repeating it.
Against
  • Both costs at once: F's engine work (two text forms to keep in step) and H's timers and hover exception.
  • Fine print in 14 px caps, the guide's floor (§12 rule 5), is the smallest text on the card.
