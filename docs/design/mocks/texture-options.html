Wood, paper and brass

Spike · spike/textures

Six ways to dress the Civic Planning Desk in real materials. Each draws the specimen's board at the game's 1920 × 1080 and a tray of loose parts (keys, a plate, a price tag, a modal) on the same desk. Nothing about layout, type or behaviour changes; only surfaces, edges and shadows. Click a card to lift it; press a key. 1:1 in the bar shows the boards at the game's own pixels, to judge the grain at the size a player sees it.

Verdict

What the spike suggests

Wood earns its place as the board, paper as every sheet, and brass only where the guide already has a "precious" mark (plates, the price tag, maybe End turn's bezel). Brass on keys or whole bands (C, D) turns the desk into a steampunk cabinet and fights the lamps.

Recommended: A for Night and B for Day, built the E way: each surface is a tiled texture under a token-coloured veil, so the veil's alpha is the one dial between "grain only" (E) and "real material" (A, B). Keep the hard offset shadows on keys and the modal; let cards on wood have the soft shadow only in Night.
