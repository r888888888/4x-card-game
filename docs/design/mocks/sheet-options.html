The screen as a sheet on the desk

These iterate on option C. The navigated screen is a sheet of paper slid over the desk. It now covers the sidebar as well as the work area, and the vertical "REALM" label is gone: the margin of desk it leaves uncovered is itself the way back. The top bar stays on top so your resources are always visible. All but option 5 stop the sheet at the hand row, so the hand and End turn stay in reach. Hover the dimmed margins to try them.

The sheet runs in from the right and stops 72 px short of the left edge, covering the work area and the sidebar down to the hand row. The Realm shows dimmed in the margin, with the edges of its first cards visible. The sheet has an ink edge, and its 8 px hard shadow falls onto the hand row, so it reads as lying on the desk.

With no label, the margin works by behaviour. The cursor turns to a hand, the Realm undims, the sheet nudges right and a tooltip says "Back to the Realm (Esc)". The sheet's own head keeps the breadcrumb and an Esc keycap.

  • + You can tell from the layout that you're off the Realm: half the familiar window is gone under paper, and the Realm peeks out from beneath.
  • + Covering the sidebar gives Knowledge's tree its full width back, which is more than C had before.
  • + End turn sits level with the hand, so it stays visible below the sheet.
  • − Without the label, the margin is a learned affordance. The first hover teaches it, but nothing invites the first hover. The breadcrumb and Esc keycap cover that gap.
  • − The civilization and government panel is hidden while the sheet is up.

Recommendation: 1, growing into 3 when screens stack

  1. One sheet, one margin. The sheet covers the work area and the sidebar down to the hand row. It leaves a 72 px margin of dimmed Realm on the left, has an ink edge, and casts its 8 px shadow onto the hand row. The hand, End turn and the top bar stay above it.
  2. The margin is the way back, with no label. It gets a pointer cursor, undims on hover, nudges the sheet 6 px as the start of its slide home, and carries a "Back to the Realm (Esc)" tooltip. The sheet's head keeps the breadcrumb and an Esc keycap for players who don't hover.
  3. Stacked screens step 48 px right and 8 px down per level (§10.6's offset sheets), and each strip goes back to its level. Cap the visible steps at two.
  4. It combines with B. The 4 px rule under the sheet's head is already in the screen's type colour. If that's not enough, fill it into the full title-block bar from the previous page.
  5. Watch: modals must still look different from navigated sheets. Modals keep shadow.sheet (8, 8) all round and a scrim, while a navigated sheet has no right edge and only casts its shadow downward.
