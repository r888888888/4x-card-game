Civilization modal

Five directions for the civilization modal (backlog 119, 205), opened from the sidebar. All show the real content of a Sumer game. The button up top swaps in a later state (Kingship, with Chiefdom and Theocracy in the government deck) so you can see how each handles more text. Mocks are at 75% scale. Pick one, or mix parts (e.g. "A, with E's unrest pips").

Now

  1. "Civilization" three times: the modal title, then "Sumer", then the type word "Civilization" right under it.
  2. No hierarchy: the modal title, the civ's name and the government's name are all the same Title size.
  3. Rules are a plain list: "When the game starts:" and "Each upkeep:" repeat, nothing lines up, and the bold "Rules" doesn't match the caps headings used elsewhere.
  4. The government is a footnote: its numbers (actions, unrest limit) are prose, the current unrest isn't shown, and Revolt… sits jammed against the last line.
  5. Flavor and quote are body-size and come before the rules, so you scroll past history to find what your civ does.

A

The ledger

The smallest change, closest to the style guide's drafting sheet. The civ's name becomes the modal title, with "Civilization" as the caps context on the right of the title bar. Flavor and quote shrink into a ruled margin note. Rules become a two-column ledger: the trigger (Start, Upkeep, Always) in a caps column, the effect beside it, so "When the game starts:" stops repeating.

The government gets its own 3 px bar and a row of stat plates: actions, and unrest shown live against its limit. Revolt… sits at the right of that row, next to the thing it overthrows.

Cost: small. Needs the engine to split each rule into trigger + effect (rules are generated, so the trigger is already known).

B

Two cards on the desk

The civilization and the government are cards, so show them as cards: two large card faces side by side, the same anatomy as the hand (title, type band, rules, flavor at the foot). The government's band is the action blue; the civ gets a band of its own.

The government deck sits underneath as a row of small card tabs, so it reads as "the cards you could switch to". Revolt… lives on the government card.

Cost: medium. Reuses CardFace at a larger size; the quote is dropped or moved to card details (click a card to open it).

C

The charter

A founding document. The guide allows centring for "single-message moments", and this is the one place the player meets their civ's identity, so the top is centred: one period motif (a small starburst), the name large in Jost, the quote as an epigraph under it.

Below, left-aligned again, the rules are numbered articles (Art. I, II…). The government is a boxed "constitution" panel with its articles and Revolt…. Flavor moves to a tooltip on the name, or is dropped.

Cost: small–medium. The most atmosphere; spends the screen's one motif here.

D

Index tabs

Uses the selectable list already chosen for the game (§7.16, the index card). Left: Sumer, Chiefdom, Government deck as tabs. Right: the selected one's details. Each pane can be roomy (full flavor, quote, rules, terms) without the modal growing tall.

The sidebar's two links open the modal on the matching tab: the civ name on Sumer, "CHIEFDOM ›" on Chiefdom. Anarchy's long text fits without scrolling. Click the tabs in the mock.

Cost: medium. Reuses SelectList; one more click to see the government from the civ name.

E

Control panel

Civ on paper, government on steel. The civ's half is a short printed sheet (name, rules, one-line flavor). The government is a recessed instrument panel: an unrest dial (the guide's circle = state) reading 2 of 5, action pips, and its extra rules underneath.

The panel is the "how stable am I" readout, so Revolt… is the panel's own control. Paper holds information, steel holds controls, as tenet 2 asks.

Cost: medium–large. A drawn dial; most useful if unrest is something players check here.
